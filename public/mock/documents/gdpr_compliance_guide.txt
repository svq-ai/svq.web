GDPR COMPLIANCE GUIDE
=====================
Version 2.1 | March 2025
Organisation: SVQ Regulatory Intelligence

1. INTRODUCTION
---------------
The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) (EU) 2016/679 is a regulation in EU law on data
protection and privacy in the European Union and the European Economic Area. It also addresses
the transfer of personal data outside the EU and EEA areas.

This guide provides a structured overview of GDPR obligations and practical compliance
recommendations for organisations that process personal data of EU residents.

2. KEY PRINCIPLES
-----------------
Article 5 of the GDPR sets out seven key data protection principles:

  (a) Lawfulness, fairness and transparency
      Processing must have a lawful basis, be fair to individuals, and be transparent about
      how data is used.

  (b) Purpose limitation
      Data must be collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further
      processed in a manner that is incompatible with those purposes.

  (c) Data minimisation
      Only data adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the purposes
      for which it is processed should be collected.

  (d) Accuracy
      Personal data must be accurate and, where necessary, kept up to date.

  (e) Storage limitation
      Personal data must be kept in a form that permits identification of data subjects for
      no longer than is necessary for the purposes for which the personal data is processed.

  (f) Integrity and confidentiality
      Appropriate security of personal data must be ensured, including protection against
      unauthorised or unlawful processing and against accidental loss, destruction or damage.

  (g) Accountability
      The controller is responsible for, and must be able to demonstrate compliance with,
      all of the above principles.

3. LAWFUL BASES FOR PROCESSING
-------------------------------
Under Article 6, there are six lawful bases for processing:

  1. Consent — the individual has given clear consent.
  2. Contract — processing is necessary for a contract with the individual.
  3. Legal obligation — processing is necessary to comply with the law.
  4. Vital interests — processing is necessary to protect someone's life.
  5. Public task — processing is necessary to perform a task in the public interest.
  6. Legitimate interests — processing is necessary for legitimate interests, unless
     overridden by the interests of the individual.

4. RIGHTS OF DATA SUBJECTS
---------------------------
GDPR grants the following rights to individuals:

  • Right to be informed (Articles 13 and 14)
  • Right of access (Article 15)
  • Right to rectification (Article 16)
  • Right to erasure — "right to be forgotten" (Article 17)
  • Right to restrict processing (Article 18)
  • Right to data portability (Article 20)
  • Right to object (Article 21)
  • Rights related to automated decision making including profiling (Article 22)

5. DATA BREACH NOTIFICATION
----------------------------
Article 33 requires that in the case of a personal data breach, the controller shall notify
the supervisory authority without undue delay and, where feasible, not later than 72 hours
after having become aware of the breach.

Article 34 requires notification to the data subject when the breach is likely to result in
a high risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons.

6. DATA PROTECTION OFFICER (DPO)
----------------------------------
Articles 37–39 require certain organisations to appoint a Data Protection Officer. A DPO
is mandatory when:

  • Processing is carried out by a public authority or body.
  • The core activities consist of processing operations that require regular and systematic
    monitoring of data subjects on a large scale.
  • The core activities consist of processing on a large scale of special categories of data
    or personal data relating to criminal convictions and offences.

7. CROSS-BORDER DATA TRANSFERS
--------------------------------
Chapter V of GDPR restricts transfers of personal data to third countries unless:

  • The European Commission has issued an adequacy decision for that country.
  • Appropriate safeguards are in place (e.g., Standard Contractual Clauses, Binding Corporate Rules).
  • A derogation under Article 49 applies.

8. PENALTIES
-------------
Infringements of the GDPR can lead to administrative fines of up to:
  • €10 million or 2% of global annual turnover for less severe violations.
  • €20 million or 4% of global annual turnover for more severe violations.

9. COMPLIANCE CHECKLIST
-------------------------
  [ ] Appoint a Data Protection Officer if required
  [ ] Maintain Records of Processing Activities (Article 30)
  [ ] Conduct Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) for high-risk processing
  [ ] Implement Privacy by Design and by Default
  [ ] Ensure lawful basis for all processing activities
  [ ] Establish processes for exercising data subject rights
  [ ] Establish breach detection and notification procedures
  [ ] Review contracts with all data processors
  [ ] Review cross-border data transfer mechanisms
  [ ] Train all staff on GDPR obligations
